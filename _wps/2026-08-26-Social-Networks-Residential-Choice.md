abstract: "Why don't more people move to places where they can earn more? Using individual-level data from Facebook, we show that social ties play a crucial role in explaining this fact. Exploiting variation in the timing of friends' moves around individuals' college graduation, we show that friends shape both whether people migrate and where they move to. Since social ties are geographically concentrated, the estimated per-friend effects translate into a large aggregate pull, helping to explain why people often remain in poorer places, and why less-educated individuals are less responsive to local economic shocks."
